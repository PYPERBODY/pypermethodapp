Before you begin

Medical & Member Safety

This guide supports the work around medication. It does not replace licensed medical care.

Educational support only. GLP-1 medications should only be used under the supervision of a licensed clinician. Always follow your prescribed dosing instructions and contact your clinician before changing medication, adding supplements, starting a new exercise plan, treating side effects, or using prescription skin actives.

What the guide does

Helps members build structure around nourishment, strength, skin care, recovery, emotional steadiness, and long-term maintenance.

What the medication may do

Quiet appetite and create a window where habits are easier to practice. The window is valuable, but it is not the entire transformation.

The PYPER tone

Elevated discipline is not punishment. It is calm, precise consistency. We are not chasing rapid loss at any cost. We are protecting muscle, supporting skin, reducing inflammation triggers, improving daily habits, and building a body you can maintain.

Your medication may start the process. Your habits protect the result.

Pillar 01

What GLP-1s Do

In everyday language, they may help reduce appetite, slow digestion, improve fullness, and make food noise feel quieter.

What they can support

  • Feeling satisfied sooner and for longer after meals.
  • Reduced hunger signals and less mental chatter around food.
  • Improved consistency with protein, hydration, and meal structure.
  • A window to rebuild habits while appetite is less dominant.

What they do not do alone

  • They do not build muscle - resistance training and adequate protein do that.
  • They do not guarantee skin tightening.
  • They do not create long-term habits automatically.
  • They do not replace clinical supervision.

PYPER Standard

Treat the medication as a window, not the whole transformation. The habits built during appetite suppression are the habits that protect maintenance later.

Medication guardrails

Dose Rhythm, Tolerance & Questions

Your provider determines medication, dose, pace, contraindications, and changes.

Medication reminders

Follow your prescription exactly. Do not double dose, accelerate dose changes, or restart after a gap without clinician guidance.

Bring to check-in

Dose day, missed doses, appetite level, side effects, hydration, constipation, reflux, mood, training, and body-skin changes.

Side-effect tracking language

Members should track symptoms plainly and early. Nausea, constipation, reflux, abdominal pain, dehydration, dizziness, mood changes, and medication uncertainty belong in the clinician conversation. Do not wait for a scheduled check-in if symptoms feel serious.

Quiet appetite is useful. Silent symptoms are not. Track early, ask early, adjust safely.

Pillar 02

The Speed Problem

Faster is not always better. Rapid weight loss can increase the risk of muscle loss, facial volume changes, fatigue, hair shedding, loose-skin appearance, and poor recovery. PYPER prioritizes steady body-composition change over dramatic scale drops.

Target pace: A commonly used target during active transformation is a steady, sustainable rate of loss, often around 1-2 lb per week for many people. Your clinician may adjust this based on your starting point, medical history, side effects, and goals.

Why muscle matters

  • Supports resting metabolic rate and functional strength.
  • Gives the body shape as fat decreases.
  • Supports posture, stability, and long-term independence.
  • Can make maintenance easier after appetite returns.

Body-composition rules

  • Protein first at every meal.
  • Strength training is scheduled, not optional, unless your clinician advises otherwise.
  • Do not undereat just because appetite is low.
  • Hydration, electrolytes, fiber, and sleep are part of the plan, not extras.
  • Use measurements, photos, clothing fit, energy, and strength - not scale weight alone.
Protein framework

A practical target many members discuss with their clinician is approximately 0.8-1.0 gram of protein per pound of target body weight daily. This is a framework, not a medical prescription. Adjust based on kidney function, dietary restrictions, history, and clinician guidance.

Weekly body-composition check

Do not wait for the scale to explain the full story. Review the pattern once weekly, then bring the trend to check-ins.

PYPER METHODMEMBER GUIDE
Pillar 03

The PYPER Nutrition Standard

The goal is to reduce decision fatigue and protect results.

Prioritize

  • High-quality protein at every meal: eggs, poultry, fish, lean beef, Greek yogurt, cottage cheese, tofu, or clinician-approved options.
  • Vegetables daily, especially leafy greens, cruciferous vegetables, peppers, cucumber, zucchini, asparagus, and green beans.
  • Healthy fats in intentional portions: avocado, olive oil, nuts, seeds, ghee, or grass-fed butter if tolerated.
  • Smart carbs when useful for training, energy, or maintenance: sweet potato, quinoa, fruit, certified gluten-free oats, or approved choices.

Reduce or avoid during active transformation

  • Sugary foods and drinks, especially liquid sugar and desserts that trigger cravings.
  • Fried foods and heavy meals that can worsen nausea or reflux.
  • Highly processed packaged foods with long ingredient lists.
  • Alcohol, especially during dose escalation or active fat-loss phases.
  • Foods that personally worsen digestion, bloating, skin, cravings, or energy.

This is not about moralizing food. It is about choosing foods that make the medication easier to tolerate and the transformation easier to maintain.

Hydration + digestion

Start Early, Not After Symptoms

GLP-1s can slow gastric emptying, which may increase constipation, nausea, reflux, and early fullness.

Daily hydration rhythm

  • Start the morning with water before coffee.
  • Sip steadily throughout the day instead of chugging large amounts at once.
  • Add sugar-free electrolytes when active, sweating, traveling, or feeling depleted.
  • Pair fiber with sufficient water to avoid worsening constipation.

Digestive support basics

  • Prioritize protein that is easier to tolerate.
  • Choose cooked vegetables when raw foods feel heavy.
  • Walk after meals to support digestion.
  • Discuss fiber, magnesium, stool softeners, or anti-nausea support with your clinician if needed.

Constipation warning: Do not normalize severe constipation, inability to hydrate, significant abdominal pain, or repeated vomiting. These are clinician conversations.

On the move

Travel Protocols

Travel should not erase the system. Make the default choices easy before hunger, delays, or social pressure take over.

Road trips

  • Pack a cooler with cooked protein, eggs, Greek yogurt, vegetables, nuts, and water.
  • Stop every 3-4 hours to walk and eat slowly.
  • Use gas stations for water, ice, and bathroom breaks - not as your meal plan.

Flights

  • Carry protein you tolerate: jerky, protein packets, nuts, tuna packets, or a clean protein bar.
  • Hydrate before boarding and avoid arriving depleted.
  • Walk during layovers when possible.
  • Keep medication storage instructions and supplies organized.

Hotels

  • Request a fridge when possible.
  • Find a nearby grocery store before arrival.
  • Keep simple proteins available for morning and late night.

Social events

  • Eat protein before you arrive if the menu is uncertain.
  • Choose one intentional indulgence rather than grazing through everything.
  • Practice calm declines. You do not owe a medical explanation.

Social protocol

Events, Holidays & Family Meals

Food-centered moments need a plan that is calm, not dramatic. The goal is to stay aligned without making your body, medication, or appetite the topic of the room.

Before you go

  • Eat a protein-forward meal before the event if the menu is uncertain.
  • Bring a dish you can eat without making it the center of attention.
  • Hydrate before alcohol, travel, or long dinners.
  • Decide what is worth it before you are tired, hungry, or pressured.

At the event

  • Choose protein and vegetables first.
  • Use simple responses: "No thank you," "I already ate," or "That does not work for me today."
  • Attend for people and memories, not pressure to eat or drink.
  • In Lifestyle Phase, choose intentional flexibility instead of grazing.
Social pressure script

No thank you is a complete sentence. You do not owe a debate about your body, your medication, your appetite, or your food choices.

Alcohol note: Alcohol can worsen nausea, reflux, dehydration, poor sleep, cravings, and medication tolerance for some members. Use clinician guidance, especially during dose escalation or active fat-loss phases.

Strength training is scheduled, not optional, unless your clinician advises otherwise.

Progressive overload

Track your lifts. When form is strong and the same weight feels manageable for multiple sessions, gradually increase reps or weight. Small progression builds muscle over time.

Movement

Pilates + Low-Impact Cardio

Pilates complements strength training. It is not a replacement for progressive loading, but it can refine how the body moves and feels.

Reformer Pilates

  • 2-3 sessions weekly if recovery allows.
  • Prioritize control over intensity.
  • Useful for posture, glutes, core, and low-impact strength.

Mat Pilates

  • Good for travel, home consistency, and core control.
  • Use it as a bridge on low-energy weeks.
  • Do not let it crowd out strength sessions if muscle preservation is the goal.

Walking

  • 10-20 minutes after meals can support digestion and glucose steadiness.
  • Daily steps build consistency without overloading recovery.
  • Use walking as nervous-system support, not punishment.

Recovery cardio

  • Zone 2 cardio can support health and endurance.
  • Avoid using intense cardio to compensate for eating.
  • Energy, sleep, and strength performance tell you if volume is too high.

Routine

The PYPER Body-Care Routine

Clinical, below-the-neck, and concern-led. Prescription actives should be used only as directed by the prescribing clinician.

AM routine

  • Cleanse with a gentle, fragrance-free body wash.
  • Pat dry. Do not aggressively rub.
  • Apply antioxidant or brightening support where appropriate.
  • Use SPF 30+ on exposed skin every day.

PM routine

  • Cleanse after sweating or training.
  • Apply prescribed body treatment only to directed areas.
  • Wait as instructed before layering moisturizer if actives irritate.
  • Use barrier support on dry, peeling, or sensitive areas.

Treatment discipline

  • Do not use more product to speed results.
  • Do not apply strong actives to folds or sensitive areas unless directed.
  • Pause and contact your clinician if severe irritation, rash, swelling, burning, or signs of infection occur.

Lifestyle support

  • Reduce friction where possible.
  • Change sweaty clothes quickly.
  • Use gentle laundry products if irritation-prone.
  • Protect sleep, protein, hydration, and healthy fats.

Recovery

Sleep, Stress & Cortisol

Poor sleep and chronic stress can increase cravings, reduce recovery, worsen mood, affect hormones, and make training feel harder.

Recovery standards

  • Aim for a consistent sleep and wake schedule when possible.
  • Protect 7-9 hours of sleep as a serious health behavior.
  • Limit caffeine later in the day if it affects sleep.
  • Use breathwork, walking, journaling, therapy, or quiet time as nervous-system support.

Signs recovery is slipping

  • Training performance drops for multiple sessions.
  • Mood becomes more reactive or flat.
  • Cravings return with poor sleep or stress.
  • Constipation, headaches, or dehydration become patterns.

Reframe

Recovery is not passive. It is the environment where body composition, skin quality, hormones, mood, and adherence can improve.

Tolerance support

Nausea, Constipation & Hair Shedding

Common issues should be tracked early and discussed before they become patterns. This page gives members language for the check-in, not instructions to self-treat.

Nausea management

  • Eat smaller meals more frequently.
  • Avoid greasy, fried, and very heavy meals.
  • Do not lie down immediately after eating.
  • Try bland protein, cold foods, ginger, or peppermint tea if tolerated.
  • Contact your prescriber if nausea is severe, persistent, or prevents hydration.

Constipation pattern

  • Review water, electrolytes, fiber, walking, meal volume, and medication timing.
  • Pair fiber with enough fluid.
  • Bring bowel frequency, pain, bloating, and vomiting notes to the clinician.
  • Do not normalize severe or prolonged constipation.

Hair shedding

  • Rapid loss, low protein, stress, iron, thyroid, vitamin D, B12, and zinc may contribute.
  • Avoid aggressive calorie restriction.
  • Ask about labs if shedding is persistent or extreme.

Appetite too low

  • Use scheduled meals instead of waiting for hunger.
  • Lean on tolerated liquid nutrition when needed.
  • Flag inability to meet protein, hydration, or medication tolerance goals.

Mindset

Mental Health + Body Image

Rapid body change can bring confidence, but it can also bring anxiety, comparison, fear of regain, body dysmorphia, and mood changes.

Seek support immediately: Contact your clinician or emergency resources if you experience suicidal thoughts, severe depression, inability to function, panic symptoms, or concerning mood changes. Do not wait for a scheduled check-in if symptoms feel serious.

Body image standards

  • Limit before-and-after scrolling if it causes comparison.
  • Use monthly photos as objective data, not punishment.
  • Do not let the scale become the only evidence of progress.
  • Notice strength, clothing fit, energy, skin tolerance, and consistency.

Identity transition

  • You may need time to adjust to a changing body.
  • Compliments can feel complicated.
  • Fear of regain is common and should be discussed.
  • Maintenance is not the end of support.

Beyond medication

Maintaining Beyond GLP-1

Appetite may return when medication is reduced or stopped. This does not mean you failed. It means the training phase is over and your systems need to hold.

Maintenance defenses

  • Protein remains the anchor.
  • Strength training remains metabolic protection.
  • Meal structure prevents grazing and decision fatigue.
  • Sleep and stress management reduce cravings and impulsive eating.
  • Check-ins catch relapse patterns early.

Transition signals

  • Weight trend stabilizes.
  • Training is consistent.
  • Meals feel repeatable.
  • Skin routine is tolerated.
  • Side effects are manageable or resolved.
  • You can handle travel, restaurants, and busy weeks without losing the thread.

Maintenance is not a softer phase. It is the skill that proves the transformation belongs to you.
